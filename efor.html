Case study / Efor Lojistik

From a customs rejection to a new way of tracking the business

Four months inside a logistics SME, and two habits the owner picked up before I left.

Role
Business & Digitalization Research Intern
Where
Konya
When
Feb–Jun 2026
  • Customer insight
  • Process improvement
  • AI adoption
  • Excel

The setting

I spent 731 hours inside a multi-sector logistics SME serving five industries, observing daily operations and interviewing the owner and 5–10 stakeholders. I wanted to understand how a small firm solves problems and adopts new tools without formal training or written procedures.

The case that stayed with me

A shipment to the Netherlands went out on pallets made from wood that had not been heat-treated. Dutch customs refused the pallets on arrival, and the whole consignment had to be re-palletized on the spot at substantial extra cost. From then on, the firm advised its clients on European regulatory requirements. The owner also set a precautionary rule against wooden crates and pallets for shipments to the Americas, where similar restrictions apply.

What I proposed

Most of the firm’s knowledge lived in moments like this one, carried in people’s heads. I recommended structured revenue and job tracking in Excel, and using AI to check regulatory changes by country and look up similar past cases before a shipment goes out.

Result

Excelrevenue and job tracking adopted by the owner
AIused for regulatory and past-case research

Before my internship ended, the owner was already finding it much easier to keep track of the business.

The most useful knowledge in the company came from specific failures that people turned into rules. A good system has to catch those moments early.
